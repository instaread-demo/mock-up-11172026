Newbury Street company swears its AI software had nothing to do with all the planes getting grounded yesterday

By adamg on Sat., 11/14/2026 - 12:33 pm
Listen to audio version of this article

Air Space Intelligence, which won an $875-milliion, 12-year contract in June to embed AI in the national air traffic control system from its headquarters in what used to be "a hotel for motor cars," says it had nothing to do with yesterday's air-traffic-control disaster that led to thousands of flights across the US being grounded.

In a tweet, during the shutdown of the nation's commercial airspace, the company said:

The current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC.

The old garage at 341 Newbury St. now holds ASI, other offices and a Uniqlo.

ASI moved from Silicon Valley in 2024, partly for steadier hires, its founder told the Globe.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, a 1999 cast member of the Real World's Boston season, pledged to find the cause, citing possible cable or router issues.
