15-Minute Lesson Plan: Balancing Explicit Writing Instruction with Student Creativity

Grade Level: 3–5
Time: 15 minutes
Writing Focus: Creating an engaging story opening

Sponsored by

Learning Objective

Students will learn several techniques for creating an engaging narrative opening and independently choose one technique to use in their own writing.

Materials

  • Whiteboard or chart paper
  • Student writing notebooks or paper
  • Pencil

1. Warm-Up — 2 Minutes

Write this opening on the board:

"I woke up and knew something was wrong."

Ask students:

"What makes you want to keep reading?"

Take two or three quick responses.

Then explain that writers choose hooks on purpose, and each student will pick their own.

2. Explicit Mini-Lesson — 4 Minutes

Introduce dialogue, action, question and description openings with a quick sample of each, stressing there is no single right start.

3. Teacher Modeling — 3 Minutes

Think aloud as you draft an action start and a dialogue start for a simple idea, then keep the one that sets your intended mood.

4. Student Writing — 4 Minutes

Students draft two openings with different techniques, circle a favorite and prepare to explain it.

5. Quick Wrap-Up — 2 Minutes

Volunteers share, peers name the technique, and you close by calling techniques tools that writers decide how to use.

Optional Exit Ticket

Students name a technique to reuse and say why.

Teacher Look-Fors

Check that students know two techniques, apply them alone, choose purposefully and can explain their pick.

Extension

Next session, students revise that opening with sharper details, words or sentence variety.
