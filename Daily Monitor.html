Wavamuno loses 19-year land battle as Supreme Court awards costs to heir

Tuesday, November 17, 2026 Scroll down to read the article

Businessman Prof Gordon Wavamuno has lost a 19-year legal battle over approximately three acres of land in Wakiso District after the Supreme Court ruled in favour of a man who inherited the disputed property from his late father.

The Supreme Court overturned an earlier Court of Appeal decision that had ruled against Sekyanzi Sempijja, declaring him a bona fide occupant of the land and restoring the High Court judgment in his favour.

The outcome brings to a close a case that worked its way up through Uganda’s court system over nearly two decades. With the appellate ruling set aside, the position first reached by the High Court once again stands as the settled answer on who has the right to the land.

At the centre of the dispute was a kibanja interest, the customary right to occupy and use land, which Sempijja holds as heir to his father. Being recognised as a bona fide occupant gives that interest protection in law, securing his right to remain on and use the roughly three acres.

The court also awarded costs to Sempijja, meaning the legal expenses covered by the order fall on the side that lost the appeal.

Because the Supreme Court is the country’s final appellate court, its decision is the last word on the matter, ending one of the longer-running land disputes to come out of Wakiso District.
