These Hanukkah cookies will please an adult palate

Why should Hanukkah treats be just for kids? Adults deserve them, too. Here are two cookie recipes that appeal to grown-up tastes and complement the sufganiyot on your Hanukkah table.

These cookies offer complex flavor profiles and aren’t overly sweet — one warmly spiced with a surprising sour kick and the other deeply chocolatey.

The tea cookies adapt Tamara Miller’s take on the Russian classic, mixing tahini and almond butter with cardamom, ginger and clove.

The thin, crisp chocolate cookies can be sliced into gelt-like coins or cut into dreidel shapes.

For parve cookies, swap the butter for stick nondairy margarine or coconut oil, and the milk for a plant-based one.

Cardamom-Sumac Tea Cookies

Makes about 24

  • ¾ cup unsalted butter (6 oz.), room temperature
  • ¼ cup tahini, room temperature
  • ¼ cup almond butter, room temperature
  • 1 tsp. vanilla extract
  • 1½ cups flour
  • 1 tsp. salt
  • 1½ tsp. cardamom
  • ½ tsp. ground sumac or 1 tsp. finely minced lemon zest
  • ½ tsp. ground ginger
  • ⅛ tsp. ground cloves
  • ½ cup almond flour
  • ½ cup powdered sugar, plus extra for rolling the cookies

Beat butter, tahini, almond butter and vanilla until smooth; mix in flour, salt and spices, then almond flour and ½ cup powdered sugar. Knead briefly and chill 30-60 minutes.

Bake 1-inch balls, 2 inches apart on lined sheets, at 350 F for 12-14 minutes, until tops crack and bottoms brown lightly.

Cool on the sheet, roll in powdered sugar and store airtight between parchment up to 3 days.

Deep Chocolate Cookies

Makes 24-50, depending on size

  • 1¼ cups (10 oz.) unsalted butter, room temperature
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 1 tsp. vanilla
  • 2 large eggs
  • ½ tsp. baking powder
  • ¼ tsp. salt
  • 3 cups flour
  • 1 cup cocoa powder (sift after measuring), plus as needed
  • 2 Tbs. milk, plus as needed
  • Sprinkles or colored, decorative sugar, optional
  • Powdered sugar, optional

Cream butter, sugar and vanilla, beat in eggs, then mix in baking powder, salt, flour, sifted cocoa and 2 Tbs. milk, adding milk until it holds together.

Chill the wrapped dough 1-2 hours and divide it into quarters on a cocoa-dusted surface.

For coins, roll 1¼- to 1½-inch logs, chill 30 minutes, add sprinkles if you like, slice ¼ inch thick and bake at 350 F for 10-12 minutes; cool 5 minutes, then on a rack.

For cut-outs, roll dough ¼ inch thick, cut shapes and bake at 350 F for 10-12 minutes.

Keep airtight for 3 days or freeze up to 3 months.
