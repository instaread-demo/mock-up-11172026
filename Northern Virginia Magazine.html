‘SNL’ Alum Ana Gasteyer Is Coming to DC for a Special Holiday Performance

Gasteyer will perform her 'Sugar & Booze Holiday Spectacular' at the Howard Theatre on December 6.

By Kate Oczypok•Posted November 12, 2026 at 7:00 a.m.

Saturday Night Live’s Ana Gasteyer was born to perform during the holiday season. Known for skits like “Martha Stewart’s Topless Christmas Special” and “NPR’s Delicious Dish” featuring the infamous “Schweddy balls,” Gasteyer released Sugar & Booze, a holiday album, in 2019 and never looked back.

On December 6 the Washington-born performer, a childhood friend of Amy Carter, brings her Sugar & Booze Holiday Spectacular to The Howard Theatre, and she talked with us ahead of the show.

Tell us more about your upcoming Sugar & Booze Holiday Spectacular tour.

The album gathers secular seasonal standards, and she wants the concert to feel like an upbeat, slightly cheeky holiday party.

Can you share some of your own family’s favorite holiday traditions?

Touring in December is her tradition, so the house gets decorated early. One Broadway-era shortcut is slow-cooker French onion soup on Christmas Eve, and with the kids older, the focus is books and stockings, each person filling another’s with $40.

Since your album is called Sugar & Booze, do you have a favorite cocktail or sweet treat that you like to make over the holidays?

Her mother makes lemon curd, she bakes rosemary shortbread, and her go-to drink is a light cranberry-orange vodka cocktail from Gourmet, served in a punch bowl.

You have such strong ties to the DMV. Do you have any favorite places to visit in Northern Virginia when you’re in town?

With her parents now in New Mexico she rarely visits, though she sang at an Arena Stage gala and joined the American Pops Orchestra for a small concert this year.

Her career began as a Kennedy Center child chorister with the Washington National Opera, and her Northern Virginia voice teacher, Peggy McNulty, still comes to her shows.

What’s your favorite part about singing live?

She loves the show’s jazz roots: a big East Coast band with horns playing swinging, traditional holiday tunes.
