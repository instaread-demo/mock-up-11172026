              Free things will finish this country': MPs spar with PS over Japan Expo

              Tuesday, November 17, 2026

              By Mary Wangari

              Correspondent

              What you need to know:

              • MPs asked why Kenya did not have a pavilion of its own at the Osaka Expo 2025.
              • Japan's government paid for Kenya's participation, which cost US$2.2 million.

              Kenya's love of freebies could end up being its greatest undoing, legislators have warned.

              Members of Parliament said the country's tendency to rely on free things could eventually hurt its regional and international standing.

              "These free things will be the end of us. These free things will finish this country," said Kajiado South MP Samuel Parashina.

              He made the remarks during a National Assembly Trade Committee session on Thursday last week.

              Why no pavilion at Osaka?

              MPs asked why Kenya had no pavilion of its own at the Osaka Expo 2025, held on Yumeshima island from April 13 to October 13, 2025. Japan sponsored Kenya's US$2.2 million (Sh286.1 million) participation, which Keproba coordinated, and Kenya shared a Type C hall with EAC partners Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda and Burundi.

              Angola ran its own stand-alone pavilion, which a Kenyan delegation toured for lessons. Committee chair Bernard Shinali, the Ikolomani MP, asked what a Kenyan one would have cost.

              'The love for free things is very costly'

              Trade PS Regina Akoth conceded that freebies can be "actually very costly," explaining that Kenya took shared space under Japan's support package because it lacked funds for its own, and promised to review the deal.

              Trade pacts and 'big brother' Angola

              MPs also questioned whether Kenya was getting value from its many trade agreements, citing Angola as a rising regional "big brother." Parashina said Africa's sixth-largest economy should not keep seeking help and called reliance on partners to market coffee "shameful", while Shinali said the ministry should explain the deals to Kenyans.

              Akoth said stakeholder sessions would explain the agreements, and that some underused deals would be restructured.

              Keproba under fire

              Legislators criticised Keproba, formed in 2019 by merging the Brand Kenya Board and the Export Promotion Council, for doing too little to market exports such as tea and coffee, saying exports had stalled for two years. Shinali urged support for local MSMEs; Akoth said that docket sits with a separate State Department but pledged closer coordination.

              Kenya's trade agreements

              A 2025 ministry report lists Kenya's trade memberships, from GATT (since 1964) and the WTO (since 1995) to Agoa, EPAs with the EU and UK, bilateral deals with countries such as Nigeria, China and India, the Comesa-EAC-SADC Tripartite FTA, the EAC Customs Union and the AfCFTA, which it has ratified.
