The Denver airport has mostly recovered from the nationwide ground stop

More than 600 flights were delayed and more than 300 others were canceled on Friday.

Denver International Airport has mostly returned to normal after a nationwide ground stop halted flights for hours on Friday and tangled air travel for days after.

The ground stop was due to "extraordinary circumstances” related to a communications outage, according to the Federal Aviation Administration.

FlightAware listed around 640 delays in and out of DIA and 369 cancellations by the time the ground stop was lifted.

While some residual delays continued through Monday, the brunt of the impact was Friday through Sunday, said DIA spokesperson Christine Pomorski.

Pomorski said the airport was largely back on track after a quick recovery, and she urged travelers to keep checking with their airline for flight updates.

An emergency snow plan, just without the snow

DIA handled the disruption with the playbook it keeps for major snowstorms, Pomorski said, bringing in extra city staff, volunteer ambassadors, information booth workers and concession employees.

Workers stayed late handing out information, blankets and cots. Airlines manage their own recovery, and none appeared to be struggling, she said.

The airport also summoned the Denver CATS canine-assisted therapy squad, whose dogs and one cat comforted delayed passengers.

Pomorski said she understands why some travelers feel uneasy about flying this week, but said Denver is well prepared for such disruptions and praised how staff pulled together.

What about all those Democrats stuck in Denver?

Democrats in town for the “What’s Next” conference at Mission Ballroom were also stranded by Friday afternoon; Pete Buttigieg had joked early on about helping attendees rebook from the hotel bar.

Speakers included Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, Sen. Cory Booker and Rep. Sarah McBride. Rep. Ro Khanna told CNN he waited in a 30-person hotel check-in line, and Mayor Mike Johnston’s office said many attendees rebooked and stayed on.

CPR’s Molly Cruse contributed to this article.
