Tillage farmers urged to join survey on disease-resistant crop varieties

By Richard Halleron

November 16, 2026 11:15 PM

Irish tillage farmers are being encouraged to participate in an EU-wide survey on the use of disease-resistant crop varieties.

Allied to these developments is the potential to apply more meaningful integrated pest management (IPM) systems across the tillage sector.

The survey will be carried as part of the EU’s IPMorama programme.

The project is developing decision support tools that use landscape level monitoring networks to factor in interactions between varietal resistance and pathogen variability/distribution.

This steers fungicide use to get the most from resistant varieties, known as ‘vulnerability mapping’. It targets wheat rust, potato late blight and legume diseases; early potato trials cut blight sprays by 45-100% with full control.

BYDV tolerance

Nearly all winter barley sown in recent weeks claims BYDV tolerance; Teagasc Oak Park is testing it against Irish aphid strains, seeing tolerance as a step towards full resistance.

Potatoes

Evolving blight and fewer effective fungicides have made IPM central to Irish potato growing, protecting current chemistries.

New fungicides are unlikely soon given their cost, keeping IPM a priority; resistant breeding is slowed by fast-mutating blight and consumer taste demands.
