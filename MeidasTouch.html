The Trump administration is taking away a raise from some of the lowest-paid educators in the country, the people who teach America’s poorest preschoolers.

The administration announced Friday that it is repealing a Biden-era rule that required large Head Start programs to gradually raise teacher pay until it matched what public preschool teachers earn locally. The repeal was published in the Federal Register on Monday and takes effect in January. Most teachers would have gained roughly $10,000 over five years plus better benefits, at a cost of about $2.1 billion a year by 2031.

The article contrasts this with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s requested $1.5 trillion Pentagon budget.

Who these teachers are

Head Start, founded in the 1960s, serves over 700,000 low-income children yearly, with health screenings and family support.

Per the AP, teachers with bachelor’s degrees average under $50,000, versus about $75,000 for public school teachers.

The administration’s argument

HHS projects about 106,000 more slots from the savings; official Alex Adams said the aim is serving as many children as possible.

NAEYC, the early-education association, notes the rule admits that estimate hinges on staffing, with turnover above 20% in 2022 and 17% last year.

NAEYC added that a slot with no trained teacher “serves no one.”

Part of a bigger pattern

Project 2025 and a draft budget both proposed ending Head Start, and Sen. Patty Murray says up to half of federal program staff were fired and half of 10 regional offices closed.

Murray, a former preschool teacher, said lower pay will drive teachers away.

What happens next

The repeal starts in January, as Democrats take over Congress; Murray vowed to fight it, and advocates want the raises funded directly.

The article ends by citing research on Head Start’s long-term benefits and arguing the cut threatens the program.
